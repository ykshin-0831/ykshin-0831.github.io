Papers

Risk Disclosure Quality and Stock Liquidity

Evidence from Embedding-Based Textual Analysis of Korean Unstructured Corporate Filings

Yeong Kyeong Shin and Jangho Gil. Under external peer review at Asia-Pacific Journal of Accounting & Economics, submitted August 2026.

Research questionDesignFindingsOngoing
My role
First author. I wrote the pipeline and ran the full-population analysis.
Data
32,314 annual reports (FY2011 to 2024) from OpenDART, 1.08 million risk sentences, and daily Korea Exchange trading records
Methods
Korean NLP (KSS, OKT), KoSimCSE embeddings, character n-gram similarity, panel regression with firm-clustered errors, split-sample validation, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR

Research question

How broad, renewed and firm-specific is the risk disclosure of Korean listed firms, and is it reflected in stock liquidity, filing-day trading, future volatility and distress delistings?

Design

Pipeline

1Collect32,314 reports via OpenDART, delisted firms included
2FilterKSS, OKT, 120-term lexicon: 1.08M risk sentences
3Repetitionexact and 4-gram Jaccard vs. firm’s t−1
4ClassifyKoSimCSE, 9 seed-anchored categories
5Measureentropy, novelty, RDQI per firm-year
6Testfirm-year panel, pre-specified A/B tests, BH

The filter runs as a resumable multiprocess job with per-worker SQLite outputs (about 9 hours for 32,314 reports on a laptop). A refactored library has parity tests against the original code paths, and the repository runs its tests in CI.

Measures

Repetition

Sentences are normalized and compared with the firm’s own sentences from the previous year, first exactly and then by Jaccard similarity of their character 4-gram sets AA and BB:

J(A,B)=∣A∩B∣∣A∪B∣,near-repeat  ⟺  J>0.8J(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|},\qquad \text{near-repeat}\iff J>0.8
J(A,B)≤min⁡(∣A∣,∣B∣)max⁡(∣A∣,∣B∣)J(A,B)\le\frac{\min(|A|,|B|)}{\max(|A|,|B|)}

The set-size bound skips most pairs without any set algebra. Run on the archived corpus, the re-implemented measure reproduces the original pipeline’s repetition rates to within 0.1 percentage point.

Classification

KoSimCSE embeddings are stored as float16 memory maps. 27 seed sentences define nine category centroids, which are re-estimated once with weights cos⁡2\cos^{2}; a 0.35 cosine floor applies to the seed stage and the weights, and every sentence then takes its nearest refined centroid (99.66% of final cosines are at least 0.35). Re-running the procedure reproduces the archived labels for 99.98% of sentences. I compared the encoder with BGE-M3 and OpenAI text-embedding-3-small.

Disclosure quality index

With pkp_{k} the share of a firm-year’s risk sentences in category kk:

H∗=−1ln⁡9∑k=19pkln⁡pkH^{*}=-\frac{1}{\ln 9}\sum_{k=1}^{9}p_{k}\ln p_{k}
novelty=1−1n∑s=1n1{s near-repeats}\text{novelty}=1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\mathbb{1}\{s\ \text{near-repeats}\}
RDQI=12 z(H∗)+12 z(novelty)\mathrm{RDQI}=\tfrac12\,z(H^{*})+\tfrac12\,z(\text{novelty})

Inference

Outcomes are regressed on the disclosure measures with year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by firm:

yi,t+1=αt+β RDQIit+γ⊤Xit+εity_{i,t+1}=\alpha_{t}+\beta\,\mathrm{RDQI}_{it}+\gamma^{\top}X_{it}+\varepsilon_{it}

Firms are hashed into halves A and B. Each pre-specified test runs on A, is re-run on B only when ∣tA∣≥1.96|t_A|\ge 1.96, and counts as confirmed only when the same term repeats sign and significance on B. Benjamini–Hochberg q-values are reported for the pre-specified family and for all tests. Filing-day abnormal volume is the log ratio of mean event-window volume to mean estimation-window volume, with a [+1,+2] window because DART filings carry a date but no time.
